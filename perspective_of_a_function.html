The perspective of a function

The sixth and last convexity-preserving operation of §3.2. Multiply by t, evaluate at x/t: the epigraph is simply rescaled through the pinhole map of §2.3.3 — and out fall quadratic-over-linear, relative entropy, and the Kullback–Leibler divergence.

g(x,t) = t f(x/t), t > 0 epi f — the original preserves convexity and concavity p. 89–90 · mastery: diagnose
§0where we are

The sixth and final convexity-preserving operation of §3.2 — next, §3.3 turns to conjugates. The perspective takes a function on \(\mathbf R^n\) and produces one on \(\mathbf R^{n+1}\) by scaling: multiply by \(t\), evaluate at \(x/t\). It preserves convexity — and its payoff is a family of important convex functions: quadratic-over-linear, relative entropy, and the Kullback–Leibler divergence.

§1definition
For \(f:\mathbf R^{n}\to\mathbf R\), the perspective of \(f\) is the function \(g:\mathbf R^{n+1}\to\mathbf R\), \[ g(x,t)=t\,f(x/t),\qquad \operatorname{dom}g=\{(x,t)\mid x/t\in\operatorname{dom}f,\ t>0\}. \] B&V §3.2.6 · L4552–4557

The domain requires \(t>0\) — not \(t\ge 0\): the construction divides by \(t\). Read \(g\) as "run \(f\) at the rescaled point \(x/t\), then scale the value back up by \(t\)."

!

The strict inequality \(t>0\) is part of the definition, not a technicality. At \(t=0\) the argument \(x/t\) is undefined, so \((x,0)\) is simply not in \(\operatorname{dom}g\).

§2the rule — both curvatures survive
If \(f\) is convex, then so is its perspective \(g\). If \(f\) is concave, then so is \(g\).B&V §3.2.6 · L4558–4559

Both curvatures are preserved — like composition with an affine mapping (§3.2.2), and unlike a general composition (§3.2.4), where the outer function's monotonicity decides what survives.

§3the epigraph proof — and the name

The proof is a single chain of equivalences on epigraphs. Fix \(t>0\):

\[ (x,t,s)\in\operatorname{epi}g \iff t f(x/t)\le s \iff f(x/t)\le s/t \iff (x/t,\,s/t)\in\operatorname{epi}f. \]B&V §3.2.6 · L4560–4571
epi g is convexit is an inverse image, not an image
\[ (x,t,s)\in\operatorname{epi}g \iff t\,f(x/t)\le s \]
definition. the epigraph of \(g\) is just its defining inequality \(g(x,t)\le s\).
\[ \iff f(x/t)\le s/t \]
divide by \(t>0\). the one step that uses positivity — dividing by a positive number preserves the direction of the inequality.
\[ \iff (x/t,\,s/t)\in\operatorname{epi}f \]
read it back. the pair \((x/t,s/t)\) satisfies the defining inequality of \(\operatorname{epi}f\).
\[ \operatorname{epi}g=P^{-1}\big(\operatorname{epi}f\big),\qquad P(u,v,w)=(u,w)/v \]
conclude. here \(P(x,t,s)=(x/t,s/t)\). the perspective map sends convex sets to convex sets under inverse image (§2.3.3), so \(\operatorname{epi}g\) is convex — hence \(g\) is convex. ∎
!

Note the direction: \(\operatorname{epi}g\) is the inverse image of \(\operatorname{epi}f\) under the perspective map — not its image. A classic flip, and the single most-missed detail of the section.

+

This is also where the name comes from: \(P\) is exactly the pinhole-camera map of §2.3.3. And an alternative route exists — direct verification of the defining inequality, left as exercise 3.33.

§4example 3.18 — euclidean norm squared
The perspective of the convex \(f(x)=x^{\mathsf T}x\) on \(\mathbf R^{n}\) is \[ g(x,t)=t\,(x/t)^{\mathsf T}(x/t)=t\cdot\frac{x^{\mathsf T}x}{t^{2}}=\frac{x^{\mathsf T}x}{t}, \] convex in \((x,t)\) for \(t>0\).B&V §3.2.6 · Example 3.18 · L4573–4582

Two cross-checks, each connecting to something already on the shelf:

  • §3.1.5
    It is \(\sum_i x_i^2/t\) — a sum of quadratic-over-linear functions, each convex from the catalogue, and sums of convex functions are convex (§3.2.1).
  • Ex 3.4
    It is a special case of the matrix fractional function \(x^{\mathsf T}(tI)^{-1}x\).

§5example 3.19 — negative log → relative entropy and KL
The perspective of the convex \(f(x)=-\log x\) on \(\mathbf R_{++}\) is \[ g(x,t)=-t\log(x/t)=t\log(t/x)=t\log t-t\log x, \] convex on \(\mathbf R^{2}_{++}\). This is the relative entropy of \(t\) and \(x\); for \(x=1\) it reduces to the negative entropy function \(t\log t\).B&V §3.2.6 · Example 3.19 · L4587–4624

Three consequences follow, each one step from the last:

  • vectors
    The relative entropy of two vectors, \(\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_i\log(u_i/v_i)\), is convex in \((u,v)\) on \(\mathbf R^{n}_{++}\times\mathbf R^{n}_{++}\) — a sum of scalar relative entropies, each a perspective of \(-\log\).
  • KL
    The Kullback–Leibler divergence \[ D_{\mathrm{kl}}(u,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(u_i\log\frac{u_i}{v_i}-u_i+v_i\Big) \tag{3.17}\] is convex — it is the relative entropy plus a linear function of \((u,v)\), so §3.2.1 (nonnegative sums) applies. And \(D_{\mathrm{kl}}(u,v)\ge 0\), with equality if and only if \(u=v\).
  • entropy
    Substituting \(v_i=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}u\) — a linear map of \(u\) — into the relative entropy gives \(\sum_i u_i\log\!\big(u_i/\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}u\big)\), convex in \(u\) (convex ∘ affine, §3.2.2). Its negative, \(\sum_i u_i\log\!\big(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}u/u_i\big)\), is the normalized entropy: a concave, homogeneous function of \(u\), equal to \((\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}u)\) times the entropy of the normalized distribution \(z=u/\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}u\).
!

Mind the sign flip in that last item: the normalized entropy is minus the \(v_i=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}u\) relative entropy, which is exactly why it comes out concave rather than convex.

§6example 3.20 — perspective composed with an affine map
Suppose \(f:\mathbf R^{m}\to\mathbf R\) is convex, and \(A\in\mathbf R^{m\times n}\), \(b\in\mathbf R^{m}\), \(c\in\mathbf R^{n}\), \(d\in\mathbf R\). Then \[ g(x)=(c^{\mathsf T}x+d)\,f\!\Big(\frac{Ax+b}{c^{\mathsf T}x+d}\Big), \] \[ \operatorname{dom}g=\Big\{x\ \Big|\ c^{\mathsf T}x+d>0,\ \frac{Ax+b}{c^{\mathsf T}x+d}\in\operatorname{dom}f\Big\} \] is convex.B&V §3.2.6 · Example 3.20 · L4627–4633

Two named operations stacked: take the perspective of \(f\) — with \(t=c^{\mathsf T}x+d\) and argument \(Ax+b\) — then precompose with an affine map. Convexity follows from the perspective rule plus §3.2.2.

+

The domain condition \(c^{\mathsf T}x+d>0\) is the \(t>0\) of §1, pulled back through the affine map. It is a strict linear inequality — an open halfspace — so the domain stays convex.

§7expert lens — what to keep straight (diagnose)

D1The construction

\(g(x,t)=t f(x/t)\), domain needs \(t>0\) (the division). Preserves both convexity and concavity.

D2Epigraph proof — inverse image

\((x,t,s)\in\operatorname{epi}g \iff (x/t,s/t)\in\operatorname{epi}f\), so \(\operatorname{epi}g\) is the inverse image of \(\operatorname{epi}f\) under the perspective map of §2.3.3. Inverse image, not image — a classic flip.

D3Payoffs to recognize

\(x^{\mathsf T}x/t\) (quadratic-over-linear); \(-t\log(x/t)=t\log(t/x)\) (relative entropy); \(\sum_i u_i\log(u_i/v_i)\) convex; \(D_{\mathrm{kl}}\) convex (relative entropy + linear), \(\ge 0\) with equality iff \(u=v\).

D4The composed form (Ex 3.20)

\((c^{\mathsf T}x+d)f\big((Ax+b)/(c^{\mathsf T}x+d)\big)\) is convex — perspective ∘ affine; note the condition \(c^{\mathsf T}x+d>0\).

D5Scope

Last operation of §3.2. §3.3 (the conjugate) is a separate construction, not another closure rule.

Summary · bv.3.2.6 · The perspective of a function (B&V §3.2.6)

Main points for quick review

Definition

\(g(x,t)=t f(x/t)\) on \(\{(x,t)\mid x/t\in\operatorname{dom}f,\ t>0\}\). Strictly \(t>0\).

Rule

\(f\) convex ⟹ \(g\) convex; \(f\) concave ⟹ \(g\) concave. Both curvatures survive.

Proof

\((x,t,s)\in\operatorname{epi}g \iff (x/t,s/t)\in\operatorname{epi}f\): \(\operatorname{epi}g=P^{-1}(\operatorname{epi}f)\), \(P(u,v,w)=(u,w)/v\) — the §2.3.3 perspective map. (Alt: exercise 3.33.)

Ex 3.18

\(f(x)=x^{\mathsf T}x\) ⟹ \(g(x,t)=x^{\mathsf T}x/t\), quadratic-over-linear; also \(x^{\mathsf T}(tI)^{-1}x\).

Ex 3.19

\(f=-\log\) ⟹ \(t\log(t/x)\), relative entropy. \(\sum u_i\log(u_i/v_i)\) convex; \(D_{\mathrm{kl}}\) (3.17) convex and \(\ge0\), \(=0\) iff \(u=v\); normalized entropy concave.

Ex 3.20

\((c^{\mathsf T}x+d)f\big((Ax+b)/(c^{\mathsf T}x+d)\big)\) convex on \(c^{\mathsf T}x+d>0\) — perspective ∘ affine.
